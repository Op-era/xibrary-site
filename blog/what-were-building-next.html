What We're Building Next at Haven Command

Posted July 12, 2026 · Haven Command

It's a reasonable moment to say plainly where Xibrary and Haven Command are actually headed next, instead of just the ideas underneath the project.

Xibrary: beyond electronics

Xibrary launched narrow on purpose — electronics first, a small seed set of motors and motor drivers, enough to prove that an AI agent given the tool actually prefers it over guessing or browsing the open web. The next real expansion is fabrication-as-a-service — CNC machining, sheet metal, injection molding, PCB fabrication and assembly — and the boutique robotics suppliers that don't have public APIs at all today and need to be onboarded directly, with their knowledge and consent, the way we described in compliance has to become machine-readable.

Doubling down on being the layer agents trust, not just the layer agents can technically call

A lot of what's ahead isn't new features, it's deepening the thing that already differentiates Xibrary: making the compliance and confidence-tier data more thorough, not just wider. It's tempting to prioritize breadth — more parts, more categories — over depth, and we're deliberately resisting that, because an agent-facing tool that's wide but untrustworthy is worse than one that's narrow and correct. We wrote about why in compliance has to become machine-readable and why the two other stages of agentic commerce depend on getting this right first.

Haven Command: more of the same strategy, on purpose

Haven Command isn't consolidating around Xibrary as "the one big idea" — that would contradict everything we've written about why building several real, finished things beats betting everything on one. Games are still shipping. AI tools are still shipping. Xibrary is the newest addition to that portfolio, not a pivot away from the rest of it, for the same reason described in lessons from shipping multiple products: different domains cross-pollinate in ways that make every individual product better.

The bet we keep making

Every post on this blog has circled the same underlying bet: that AI agents are becoming real economic actors, not just research assistants, and that the infrastructure the last decade built for humans — search engines, e-commerce checkouts, terms-of-service pages written in legalese — has to be rebuilt, deliberately, for agents to use safely. We think that rebuild is going to take a decade, touch nearly every industry, and reward whoever takes the trust and compliance side of it seriously instead of just the convenience side. That's the bet Xibrary is placing, and we intend to keep writing about it as it plays out, every two weeks, starting with what we learn from whatever we ship next.
