Haven Command's Philosophy: Build Small, Build Real, Ship Often

Posted February 22, 2026 · Haven Command

Haven Command doesn't have one product. It has several, and they don't obviously belong together on a slide: an AI chess trainer, an AI therapy-notes app for clinicians, a multiplayer survival game, a retro-console hosting platform, and now Xibrary, a sourcing index built for AI agents instead of people. That's not scattershot — it's the strategy.

Why build several small things instead of one big thing

The instinct in software is often to pick one idea and go all-in. We do the opposite on purpose. Building several smaller, real products — each one shipped, each one used by actual people, each one finished rather than perpetually "in progress" — teaches you things a single big bet never will. Every product has its own users, its own failure modes, its own lessons about what actually matters versus what only looks like it matters on a roadmap. A chess training tool and a therapy documentation tool have almost nothing in common on the surface, but they teach the same underlying lesson: ship something real, watch what breaks, fix it, ship again.

That habit is exactly what made a project like Xibrary possible. We didn't sit down and theorize about "the future of AI commerce." We were building things — games, tools — increasingly with AI agents doing real work inside the build process, and we kept running into the same wall: agents are excellent at reasoning and terrible at knowing where to actually get a physical part. That's not a hypothesis. It's a problem we hit directly, which is why we started building Xibrary in the first place.

Games teach you systems thinking

A meaningful chunk of what Haven Command builds is games — and game design turns out to be one of the best possible training grounds for thinking about AI agents, because both are fundamentally about designing systems with rules, feedback loops, and agents (whether that's a player or an AI) that act within them. We go deeper on that specific connection in what game design teaches you about designing for AI agents.

Using AI to build faster, not just to build differently

None of this happens at the pace it does without leaning hard on AI tooling in the actual build process — not as a novelty, but as core infrastructure for how a small studio competes with much larger teams. We've written about the specific tools and workflows in how we use AI to build faster at Haven Command.

The through-line across every Haven Command product, including this blog, is the same: build the real thing, ship it, let it meet actual use, and let that contact with reality tell you what to build next. Xibrary is the newest expression of that — a bet that the next wave of "real use" is going to come from AI agents themselves, not just the people directing them.
